# Phase 10 — Accessories

**Time:** shopping, not setup
**Risk:** to your wallet only

Scoped to your constraints: external only, nothing that opens the chassis. Ranked by actual impact
on your two use cases — handheld on battery, and docked to a TV.

---

## Buy these

### 1. A 100 W USB-C PD GaN charger — highest impact per pound spent

Covered in Phase 8, repeated here because it's the accessory most likely to fix a problem you
haven't diagnosed yet. The stock 65 W brick is marginal when docked, charging, and running a 30 W
profile with peripherals attached.

Any reputable 100 W GaN charger works — Anker, UGREEN, Baseus, Satechi. Get one with a
100 W-rated USB-C cable; the cable is often the limiting factor.

**Bonus:** it's smaller and lighter than the ASUS brick, so it's also the better travel charger.

### 2. A dock

Requirements from Phase 8: HDMI 2.1, 100 W PD passthrough, Gigabit Ethernet, a couple of USB-A
ports, and a cradle that doesn't block the top exhaust vent.

Handheld-specific docks (JSAUX and similar make several aimed at the Ally line) get the cradle
geometry and vent clearance right, and some include a fan aligned with the intake. A generic USB-C
hub is electrically fine but may sit the device badly.

### 3. An Ultra High Speed HDMI cable

Cheap, and the single most common cause of "my dock won't do 120 Hz." Buy a certified HDMI 2.1
cable rather than trusting whatever came with the TV.

### 4. A UHS-II microSD card

512 GB or 1 TB, V60/V90, from a retailer you trust. See Phase 9 — including verifying it isn't
counterfeit before you commit a library to it.

### 5. A case

The Ally X is a £700+ device with a large glass panel and exposed stick tops. ASUS's own travel case
is fine; third-party hard cases are often better value. Look for one with a divider that keeps the
sticks from pressing against the lid.

### 6. A screen protector

Tempered glass, matched to the Ally X's 7" panel. Note the Ally X's dimensions differ from the
original Ally — check the listing specifies the X.

Matte protectors reduce glare noticeably outdoors at the cost of slight sharpness. Worth considering
if you play near windows; skip if you're mostly indoors.

---

## Consider these

### A controller for docked play

Reaching across the room for the handheld defeats the point. Xbox controllers pair over Bluetooth
and Just Work. DualSense works well with Steam Input including gyro and touchpad. See Phase 8.

### A cooling stand or active-cooling grip

Genuinely helps if you play long docked sessions in a warm room — lower temperatures let the APU
hold boost clocks longer. Mostly redundant if your dock already includes a fan.

Skip for handheld use; you're holding it, and the added bulk isn't worth it.

### A power bank with 65 W+ PD

For travel. Note the Ally X's 80 Wh internal battery is already large — a 20,000 mAh bank
(~74 Wh) gives you roughly one extra charge, less conversion losses. Make sure it does **65 W or
higher PD output**; a 30 W bank will slow-charge while the device drains faster than it fills.

Check airline restrictions if you fly — 100 Wh is the usual carry-on limit for power banks.

---

## Skip these

- **"Gaming" microSD cards at a premium** — the UHS-II spec is what matters, not the branding
- **Anything advertising "8K"** on a device that shouldn't render 4K
- **Thermal pads, paste, or internal cooling mods** — outside your constraints, and the Ally X's
  stock cooling is competent
- **Screen "privacy" filters** — they cut brightness substantially on a panel that needs all of it
  outdoors
- **Docks without PD passthrough** — you'd be choosing between charging and using peripherals
- **Cheap unbranded USB-C hubs** — display and power negotiation on handhelds is fussier than on
  laptops, and this is where flaky dock behavior comes from

---

## If you only buy two things

The **100 W charger** and the **HDMI 2.1 cable**. Together they cost very little and they eliminate
the two most common docked-mode problems — problems that otherwise present as vague performance
complaints you'd spend an evening chasing through software settings.
