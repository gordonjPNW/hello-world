# RAID: Shadow Legends — Early-Game Playbook

A personal runbook for taking an early account (roughly level 30–50) to the point where it farms its
own gear, keys the Clan Boss reliably, and can actually *use* an event instead of watching it go by.
Follow the phases in order — later phases assume earlier ones are done.

## Your account

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Early game, under ~level 50 |
| **Unlocked already** | Campaign, all six dungeons, Clan Boss, Classic Arena, Faction Wars (level 30) |
| **Next gates** | Doom Tower at **level 40**, Hydra and Live Arena at **level 45–50** |
| **Priorities** | Progression roadmap, dungeon squads, Clan Boss & Hydra, events & fusions |

## Scope

- **You play the game. This guide tells you what to build and when.** Nothing here involves bots,
  scripts, auto-clickers, or third-party account tools. Plarium's terms of service prohibit them and
  enforcement is account termination — there is no version of that trade worth making.
- **No spending advice.** Where an offer is genuinely the difference between progress and a wall
  it's noted, but the whole playbook works free-to-play.
- **Role templates, not champion lists.** See below.

## How the squad tables work

Every squad in this playbook is written as **roles**, not names:

| Slot | Why it's there | Common early champions | Your pick |
|---|---|---|---|

RAID advice normally reads "run these five champions," which is useless if you don't own them — and
nobody owns the same five. So each squad lists the *jobs* that need doing, why each job matters, and
common early-game champions who do it. **Fill in the last column with what you actually have.**

The role is the durable part. If you later summon someone better for a slot, swap the name and the
team still works.

## The one idea worth internalising

**One well-built champion beats five half-built ones.** This is the mistake that costs new accounts
months: spreading food, gear, and silver across a wide roster because everything looks promising.

An early account has one real bottleneck — it cannot farm hard enough content to gear itself. The
entire early game is about breaking that bottleneck with a small number of properly built champions,
in this order:

1. A **campaign farmer** that clears a stage on auto, forever, without dying.
2. A **Dragon team** that clears Dragon 20+ so gear stops being the limiting factor.
3. A **Clan Boss team** that reliably takes chests every day.

Everything else is downstream of those three. When you're deciding where to spend a resource, ask
which of the three it advances. If the answer is "none," it can wait.

## A note on expectations, and on this guide's shelf life

RAID is a live-service game. Champion balance shifts, fusion schedules rotate monthly, and the "best
team" for any content changes with every patch. So this playbook deliberately sticks to **mechanics
that don't move** — the gear treadmill, level gates, debuff priority, speed tuning, mastery costs —
and links out to live trackers for anything dated. You won't find a fusion calendar hardcoded here,
because it would be wrong within a month.

Treat any guide promising a shortcut past the gear treadmill with suspicion. There isn't one. Dragon
20 gates the mid-game for everybody, and Phase 9 has you measure your progress toward it precisely
so you can tell real progress from the feeling of being busy.
