# Phase 1 — Identify the system

**Time:** 15 minutes. **You need:** the car, a phone camera.

Three infotainment configurations shipped in the second-gen Cruze, and they're all called "MyLink."
Getting this wrong sends you down the wrong path for every phase after this one.

## The three units

| RPO | Screen | Navigation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IOB** | 7-inch | No | Standard on LT. Wired CarPlay + Android Auto. Most likely what she has. |
| **IO5** | 8-inch | No | Optional upgrade. Same feature set, larger display, different hardware. |
| **IO6** | 8-inch | **Yes** — embedded maps | Optional. Same 8-inch hardware as IO5, plus a GM map database. |

The practical difference that matters later: **IO6 has map data that is versioned and updated
separately from radio software.** IOB and IO5 have no maps at all — they rely on the phone for
navigation, which in practice means CarPlay/Android Auto, which makes [Phase 4](04-carplay-android-auto.md)
the single most important phase for those cars.

## Read the RPO sticker

GM prints a **Service Parts Identification** label listing every option code the car was built with.
On a Cruze it's typically inside the **glovebox** — check the underside of the lid and the inner
wall. If it's not there, look in the trunk area under the cargo floor, or on a door jamb.

Photograph the whole sticker. It's a wall of three-character codes; scan for `IOB`, `IO5`, or `IO6`.

While you have it photographed, note anything else useful — it's the fastest way to settle later
arguments about what the car was actually built with.

## If the sticker is missing or unreadable

Fall back to visual identification, which is nearly as reliable here:

- **7-inch screen** → IOB. The display sits flush in the dash bezel and is noticeably narrower than
  the surrounding trim.
- **8-inch screen** → IO5 or IO6. To tell those two apart, look for a **navigation source in the
  audio/app menu**. If the car can route you to an address with no phone connected and no data, it's
  IO6. If not, it's IO5.

You can also read the VIN into a dealer's parts system or the Chevrolet Owner Center and have them
tell you the build codes.

## Also confirm CarPlay and Android Auto are actually there

On MY2017 Cruze, both were standard across trims, so an LT should have them. Confirm anyway, because
a previous owner may have had the head unit replaced:

1. Start the car and let the radio finish booting.
2. Look at the home screen icon grid. Unconnected, you should see **Apple CarPlay** and **Android
   Auto** icons — usually greyed out, sometimes with a "connect a device" prompt behind them.

No icons at all, on a car whose sticker says IOB/IO5/IO6, is a sign the unit was swapped for an
earlier-generation radio. That's worth knowing before you spend money in [Phase 7](07-upgrades.md).

## Record what you found

Write it down somewhere you'll find it again — the glovebox photo plus a note:

```
Radio RPO:        IOB / IO5 / IO6
Screen size:      7" / 8"
Embedded nav:     yes / no
CarPlay icon:     present / absent
Android Auto:     present / absent
Software version: (Phase 3 shows you where to read this)
```
