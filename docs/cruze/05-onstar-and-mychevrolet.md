# Phase 5 — OnStar & myChevrolet

**Time:** 45 minutes, most of it possibly on hold. **You need:** the VIN, and whoever's name the car
is registered in.

The 2017 Cruze shipped with OnStar hardware on **4G LTE** — which matters, because the 3G network
shutdown that bricked telematics in older GM vehicles does not affect this car. The hardware is
still on a live network.

What's live is the *hardware*. Whether the *services* are live is a separate question, and this is
where a 2017 car sits at an awkward moment in 2026.

## The Connected Access expiry — read this part

GM's free tier has changed names and terms more than once:

- Introduced for MY2015 as the **OnStar Basic Plan**, with a **five-year** term from delivery.
- Rebranded around 2018 to **Connected Access**, with the term extended to **ten years** from
  vehicle delivery.

A 2017 Cruze was delivered somewhere in the 2016–2017 window. **Ten years from delivery lands in
2026 or 2027** — meaning the free connected-services term on this car is expiring around now, if it
hasn't already.

What that affects: the remote commands in the myChevrolet app — remote start, lock/unlock, horn and
lights — plus vehicle diagnostics reporting into the app. When the term lapses, those stop working
and the app starts asking for a paid plan.

Which term this specific car got depends on when it was delivered and which plan generation it fell
under. **Don't guess. Call OnStar with the VIN and ask two questions:**

> "What connected services plan is active on this VIN, and what is its expiration date?"

> "What does the vehicle keep after that date at no cost?"

Get the expiration date in writing if you can. It changes whether a paid plan is worth it.

## Set up the app regardless

Even with no paid plan, the myChevrolet app is worth having. It supports model year 2010 and newer
Chevrolets, and the parts that don't require an active services plan still work:

- Owner's manual for the exact VIN — genuinely the fastest way to look something up
- Service history and maintenance reminders
- **Open recall lookup by VIN** — the same check as [Phase 2](02-recalls-and-bulletins.md)
- Dealer locator and service scheduling

Setup:

1. Install **myChevrolet** from the App Store or Play Store.
2. Create an account, or sign in if the car already has one attached from a previous owner.
3. Add the vehicle by VIN.
4. If the car was bought used, expect an ownership-verification step. A previous owner's account can
   remain attached to the VIN and block you. OnStar support can clear this — have proof of ownership
   ready.

That last point catches people. If the app refuses to add the VIN, it's usually a stale prior-owner
account, not a broken app.

## Remote start — know what she actually has

Two different things get called remote start:

- **Key fob remote start** — a factory hardware feature, works whether or not any subscription is
  active, no phone needed. Requires the car to have been built with it and to have an automatic
  transmission. On the fob, it's the button with the circular arrow, usually pressed after lock.
- **App remote start** — goes through OnStar's network, and **requires an active services plan**.

If she has fob remote start, an expiring Connected Access term costs her much less than it sounds
like. Test the fob before deciding whether to pay for anything: lock the car, then press and hold the
remote start button, and watch for the lights and the engine.

Manual-transmission cars don't get remote start in either form.

## The Wi-Fi hotspot

The built-in 4G LTE hotspot is a **separately purchased data plan**, and always was — it was never
part of the free tier. Buy it only if there's a real use case, like long drives with a tablet in the
back. For occasional use, phone tethering is cheaper and does the same job.

## Deciding whether to pay

Once you know the expiration date, the honest math is:

**Worth paying for** if she drives alone a lot at night or in poor weather and wants automatic crash
response and emergency calling, or if the car has no fob remote start and she'll genuinely use app
remote start through winter.

**Not worth paying for** if she has fob remote start, carries a phone with location sharing, and
mainly wanted the app for the convenience features. Everything else the app does — manual, recalls,
service — keeps working without a plan.

Stolen-vehicle assistance and automatic crash response are the two features with no phone-based
substitute. Weigh those honestly rather than by price alone.

## While you're in the account

- Check **who the account holder is**. If it's a previous owner or a dealership, fix it now, not
  during an emergency.
- Set the **emergency contacts** on the OnStar account to real, current numbers.
- If she doesn't want the diagnostics and location data collection, ask OnStar directly about
  **opting out of connected services data sharing** for the VIN. It's a real request they can
  process, and it's separate from cancelling a plan.
